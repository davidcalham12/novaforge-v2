# Outline: The Phantom Station

## Act Structure

**Three-Act Structure:** The story moves from discovery through investigation to convergence. Act 1 plants all three mysteries and introduces institutional resistance. Act 2 deepens the investigation as characters race against bureaucratic obstruction. Act 3 delivers all answers simultaneously—when the mysteries converge, the truth becomes undeniable.

---

## Chapter 1: The Sensor Reading
**Tension curve:** Opens with Maya's discovery (excitement, vindication), escalates as impossible implications become clear, peaks when three independent sources confirm the anomaly, resolves into commitment despite institutional hostility.

**Beats:**
- **Beat 1 – The Anomaly:** Maya presents her ground-penetrating radar survey to Sarah and Robert. Beneath Broadway: a complete platform structure, rails, load-bearing walls—all physically present, architecturally perfect, untouched. But there is no record of it anywhere in any official city database. Maya's credibility depends on proving this is real, not sensor error.

- **Beat 2 – The Missing Records:** Sarah reveals the GIS audit she conducted during her five years at CTA. The 1962–1963 archive is systematically absent—not scattered, not water-damaged, but cleanly removed. Permits, blueprints, inspection photos from that entire period: gone. A gap in continuity no one thought to question until she built the digital map.

- **Beat 3 – The Confirmation:** Robert, dragged reluctantly into the conversation, confirms Project 117 was real. He was present during the 1979 Great Digitization. The station was approved by City Council, formally documented. He saw the project. He never understood why it disappeared from the record.

- **Beat 4 – The Impossible Convergence:** Three streams of evidence (sensor, documentary absence, institutional memory) point to the same conclusion: the station exists physically, was approved officially, but does not appear in the present record. This violates every rule of the system. Either something was hidden, or all three sources are simultaneously unreliable. The stakes become personal for each character: Maya's methodology, Sarah's archive, Robert's judgment.

- **Beat 5 – The Commitment:** Despite Robert's warnings that the CTA will resist, despite Sarah's fear of institutional reprisal, the three agree to investigate. They know they cannot solve this in isolation. They need to find the vanished records. They need to understand why a documented station disappeared.

---

## Chapter 2: The Regulatory Limbo
**Tension curve:** Begins with renewed investigation (hope, momentum), deepens as physical evidence mounts and contradictions emerge, escalates when the characters realize someone may have deliberately concealed the records, peaks when they find proof of access to a sealed tunnel.

**Beats:**
- **Beat 1 – The Legal Shadow:** Robert explains the regulatory framework that makes the Phantom Station possible. A station that was approved but never formally decommissioned exists in legal limbo—not official, but never officially closed. To reopen it, you would need proof that the decommissioning order was never filed. To deny it, you would need the decommissioning order. The gap is not an oversight; it is a perfect concealment mechanism.

- **Beat 2 – The Disposal Search:** Sarah begins systematic work through archived disposal records and transfer logs from 1979. Thousands of documents were marked for disposal during the Great Digitization. A single form could have authorized destruction of Project 117 files. She finds disposal forms, but the authorizing signatures are sometimes illegible, dates inconsistent. Something in this paper trail will tell them who made the decision to remove the records.

- **Beat 3 – The Sealed Entrance:** Maya and Sarah locate the tunnel entrance beneath Broadway, sealed for decades. They photograph deteriorated "Do Not Enter" signs, witness marks on the concrete suggesting the barrier was recently replaced or reinforced. Someone has been maintaining this concealment. It is not forgotten; it is actively hidden.

- **Beat 4 – The Partial Proof:** Among partially recovered archival materials (worker rosters, progress photos, newspaper clippings), they find fragments: photographs of construction dated 1962–1963, payroll records showing engineers assigned to "Project 117," a newspaper article from 1963 mentioning "New Downtown Transit Hub." The station was built. Not proposed, not planned—built.

- **Beat 5 – The Escalation:** Sarah receives a quiet visit from a CTA supervisor asking why she is interested in 1979 records. The message is subtle but clear: investigate further and you risk your position. Maya realizes her credibility is on the line with every source she contacts. Robert is warned that speaking about the station contradicts his official pension record. Institutional pressure tightens, but they are now too close to stop.

---

## Chapter 3: The Convergence
**Tension curve:** Opens with final discoveries arriving rapidly (all three mysteries landing simultaneously), escalates as the full narrative emerges, peaks when the evidence becomes undeniable, resolves when institutions are forced to acknowledge.

**Beats:**
- **Beat 1 – The Deliberately Removed Records (Mystery 2 Lands):** Sarah traces the disposal order through 1979 signatures and payroll records. Richard Donnelly, a mid-level transit administrator overseeing the digitization move, authorized destruction of "obsolete 1962–1963 materials." His memo describes the files as "incomplete, superseded by current engineering standards, recommended for immediate disposal to reduce archival footprint." The memo is present; the files are not. Donnelly's deliberate action removed the documentation, creating the gap in the official record.

- **Beat 2 – The Lost Permits (Mystery 1 Lands):** Sarah discovers the original permits and blueprints in a secondary archive—a storage facility in the suburban records center, unmarked and nearly lost during a 2005 move. The documents prove Project 117 was formally approved by City Council in 1962, budgeted for $4.2 million, and construction was authorized. Every architectural drawing exists. Every inspection stamp is present. The station is not phantom; it is documented. The concealment was not of its existence but of its documentation.

- **Beat 3 – The Undecommissioned Station (Mystery 3 Lands):** Sarah searches comprehensively for decommissioning paperwork. Nothing exists. No formal closure order, no regulatory approval to abandon the station, no transfer of assets. The station was simply left sealed. In regulatory terms, it was never officially decommissioned. The gap that Donnelly created—removing the permits and approvals—created the limbo that allowed the station to exist "off the books." No decommissioning was needed because the project vanished from the record entirely.

- **Beat 4 – The Narrative Converges:** The three characters piece together what happened: Project 117 was built (physical evidence, recovered permits, worker records). Something between 1963 and 1979 prompted someone to want it forgotten. Donnelly's order destroyed its documentation to erase it from the official record. The regulatory gap—a station that was never formally closed but was erased from files—created a structure that could be maintained in secrecy for decades. The station was not hidden because it was shameful or dangerous; it was hidden because removing its documentation was the easiest way to make it officially cease to exist.

- **Beat 5 – The Recognition:** The recovered permits, blueprints, and architectural drawings, combined with Maya's sensor data and Robert's engineering testimony, force the CTA to issue a public acknowledgment. The Phantom Station is officially recognized as a built structure that exists in regulatory limbo due to archival destruction. It becomes a matter of record. Maya's methodology is vindicated; Sarah's professional integrity is restored; Robert's institutional memory is confirmed. The invisible made visible, the undocumented made evidence, the phantom becomes real.
