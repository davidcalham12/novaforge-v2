# Cast: The Phantom Station

## Maya Chen
**Age:** 42 | **Role:** Independent cartographer, protagonist

**Background:**
Fifteen years mapping the city's subway system for academic publications, historical societies, and the Independent Cartographers Guild. Self-taught GIS expert who combines satellite data, archival records, and sensor surveys to create comprehensive maps. Her reputation is built on rigorous documentation—every line on her map traces back to a primary source.

**Stakes:**
Her credibility depends on sourcing. The Phantom Station is an anomaly that shouldn't exist: sensor data shows a physical structure, but no documentation proves it was ever built or approved. If she can't prove it's real, she becomes the cartographer who chased a phantom; if she can prove it, she rewrites the city's official history.

**Relation to the Mystery:**
Discovers the anomaly during a sensor survey. Becomes the driving force behind the investigation. Must convince the CTA, city officials, and the historical record that the station exists—a task that requires finding documentation that everyone assumes was lost or never existed.

**Personality & Voice:**
Methodical, detail-obsessed, skeptical of easy answers. Sees patterns where others see noise. Impatient with institutional inertia. Speaks directly; thinks in coordinates and data points. Has a dry sense of humor about her obsession with dead infrastructure.

## Robert Hartley
**Age:** 73 | **Role:** Retired CTA senior engineer, reluctant ally

**Background:**
Forty-five-year career at the City Transit Authority, retiring fifteen years ago as a senior systems engineer. Present during the Great Digitization of 1979, when millions of documents were migrated to microfiche. Remembered by colleagues as methodical and principled—someone who followed procedure and expected the institution to do the same.

**Stakes:**
His understanding of the system is being challenged by the existence of something he never knew about. If the Phantom Station is real, it means either he missed something during his four decades in the system, or he was deliberately kept in the dark. Either way, his legacy—his confidence that he understood the infrastructure completely—is threatened. His institutional loyalty conflicts with his intellectual honesty.

**Relation to the Mystery:**
Provides crucial institutional knowledge. Confirms he remembers Project 117 vaguely ("something in the early '60s"). Explains the regulatory framework (decommissioning procedures, documentation requirements). Becomes uncomfortable when gaps in the records suggest deliberate cover-up rather than careless loss. Eventually becomes a reluctant whistleblower about what he suspects happened in 1979.

**Personality & Voice:**
Confident, institutional, precise. Speaks in terms of procedures and precedent. Can be defensive when questioned, but ultimately values truth over comfort. Treats the investigation like a systems problem—trace the inputs, follow the process, find where it broke.

## Sarah Okafor
**Age:** 47 | **Role:** CTA Archivist, whistleblower

**Background:**
Joined the CTA five years ago after fifteen years as an archival consultant for universities and museums. Hired specifically to modernize the physical archive during the installation of a new GIS system. Meticulous about cataloguing and preservation. Her first mandate: identify gaps in digitized records and assess what physical documents need preservation or scanning.

**Stakes:**
Her discovery of the missing 1962-1963 filing sections puts her in an impossible position. Pursuing the truth risks her job and her professional reputation at the institution. Covering it up betrays everything she believes in as an archivist. The institution that hired her expects compliance; her profession demands honesty. She must choose between institutional loyalty and professional integrity.

**Relation to the Mystery:**
Discovers the archival gap through routine audit procedures. Alerts both Maya and Robert. Becomes the key to uncovering what happened during the 1979 records migration. Traces document movement through warehouse logs, personnel records, and disposal manifests. Eventually discovers that the missing documents were deliberately marked for disposal by a specific CTA supervisor—a finding that transforms the mystery from lost records to institutional cover-up.

**Personality & Voice:**
Meticulous, principled, cautious but ultimately courageous. Speaks in terms of documentation and provenance. Thinks like an archivist: everything has a source, an origin, a chain of custody. Becomes increasingly uncomfortable with institutional pressure to drop the inquiry. Her turning point is when she realizes that as an archivist, she is the guardian of institutional memory—and that memory is being deliberately erased.

## Connections & Tensions

**Maya ↔ Robert:**
Maya sees Robert as institutionally blind—he missed the Phantom Station entirely. Robert sees Maya as an idealist chasing ghosts. They respect each other's rigor but distrust each other's conclusions. Over time, Robert becomes Maya's credibility—his institutional authority combined with her sensor data makes the case undeniable.

**Maya ↔ Sarah:**
Sarah has access to the archives; Maya has the evidence of the physical structure. Sarah wants to believe the documents exist somewhere; Maya wants proof they were deliberately hidden. Their partnership works because they need each other—neither can solve the mystery alone.

**Robert ↔ Sarah:**
Robert's discomfort with the 1979 gap overlaps with Sarah's discovery. He knows the digitization project; she's finding evidence it was deliberately destructive. Robert becomes Sarah's shield when the CTA pressures her to stop asking questions—his reputation protects hers long enough for the truth to surface.

**Triangle Tension:**
Robert represents institutional history; Maya represents independent truth-seeking; Sarah represents archival integrity. The three perspectives clash and align depending on what each new discovery reveals about what happened to the Phantom Station.
