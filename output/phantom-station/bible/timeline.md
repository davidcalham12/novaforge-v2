# Timeline: The Phantom Station

## 1962 — Project 117 Approved
**Date:** March 1962

The City Transit Authority secures City Council approval for Project 117: construction of a rapid-access platform station beneath Broadway in the downtown core. The project is intended to serve as a hub for cross-town express service. Formal permits are filed with the Municipal Building Department. Architectural blueprints are drawn up and construction contracts are signed. The project is assigned a budget and a timeline: completion projected for 1965. CTA engineers, including a younger Robert Hartley, are assigned to oversee the excavation and installation phases. All documents are archived in the CTA's central filing system.

**Significance:**
Establishes that the Phantom Station was real—officially approved, formally documented, and resourced. Sets up the timeline for when documents should exist and creates the baseline against which later gaps will become visible.

---

## 1963 — Project 117 Suspended
**Date:** September 1963

Construction abruptly halts. An internal memo from the CTA Board designates the project "indefinitely suspended." No public announcement is made. No formal decommissioning order is filed with the city. The memo provides no explanation—only a project number and a status change. The partially completed tunnel and platform structure remains underground, sealed but accessible through maintenance access points. Workers are reassigned. Contracts are terminated. The project enters an institutional gray zone: neither officially completed nor officially abandoned.

**Significance:**
Creates the regulatory limbo that defines the Phantom Station. The suspension without decommissioning creates a legal inconsistency: the station exists in the physical world but doesn't exist in the regulatory world. The lack of explanation hints at something concealed. Robert Hartley remembers this period vaguely but has no clear recollection of why the project was cancelled.

---

## 1979 — The Great Digitization
**Date:** April–November 1979

The City Transit Authority launches a massive project to migrate decades of paper records—blueprints, permits, contracts, payroll records, and correspondence—from climate-uncontrolled warehouses to microfiche and organized filing systems. The effort involves moving millions of documents. Several warehouse boxes from the 1960s are lost during transport between facilities. At the time, staff assume these boxes contained duplicate or outdated blueprints. No comprehensive inventory is conducted; loss is attributed to simple carelessness. Project 117 documentation—including the original blueprints, construction permits, and the unexplained suspension memo—is among the missing materials.

**Significance:**
The institutional moment when the Phantom Station's history is erased from the official record. The loss is presented as accidental, a consequence of inadequate record-keeping during a logistical challenge. However, the precision of what is lost (all Project 117 documentation from a specific time period) will later suggest the loss was deliberate. This is where institutional memory becomes fallible, and documents—once considered the unambiguous source of truth—disappear.

---

## 2025 — The Audit Begins
**Date:** January 2025

The City Transit Authority installs a new Geographic Information System (GIS) to replace legacy mapping infrastructure. As part of this modernization, Sarah Okafor is hired as head archivist to catalog all remaining physical documents and identify gaps in the digitized record. Her mandate is to cross-reference the old paper system against digital archives and flag discrepancies. Within weeks, she discovers that entire filing sections from 1962–1963 are completely absent—not scattered, not misfiled, but systematically missing. A query in the master index shows references to Project 117, but the physical documents no longer exist, and no digitized copies can be found. She escalates the discovery to CTA management, expecting explanations. She receives bureaucratic resistance instead.

**Significance:**
The moment when the cover-up becomes officially undeniable. The archival gap moves from historical accident to institutional anomaly. Sarah's discovery creates pressure on the system—someone must explain what happened. Her investigation begins to attract attention from Maya Chen, who is concurrently mapping the subway system through independent means.

---

## September 2026 — The Sensor Anomaly
**Date:** September 2026

Maya Chen completes a ground-penetrating radar survey of the abandoned tunnel corridor beneath downtown, part of her ongoing project to map the city's complete subway infrastructure. Her sensor data reveals an anomaly: 14 meters below Broadway, the instruments detect a chamber, track bed, and platform structure consistent with a subway station. The dimensions and layout match the specifications of CTA stations built in the 1960s. The structure is complete—walls, platform edge, track bed are all present and structurally intact. But when Maya cross-references this location against every available map, database, and archival record, she finds nothing. No official station exists at these coordinates. No decommissioning record explains its abandonment. No City Council minutes mention its construction. The Phantom Station becomes a physical fact without documentation—a mystery that cannot be ignored.

**Significance:**
The catalyst that brings together Maya's evidence (the physical structure), Sarah's discovery (the missing records), and Robert's institutional knowledge (the explanation of Project 117). This is the convergence event that transforms three isolated anomalies—Sarah's archival gap, Robert's vague memory of a cancelled project, and Maya's sensor data—into a single unified mystery: the Phantom Station.

---

## Why This Matters Now

Three threads converge in September 2026:
1. **Sarah's audit** has made the missing records undeniable.
2. **Maya's sensor survey** has proven something physical exists where documentation says nothing should.
3. **The GIS system installation** creates an institutional moment when the city must decide what the official map actually contains.

The book begins at this convergence—the moment when three independent investigations force the city to confront a secret it has kept for sixty years.
